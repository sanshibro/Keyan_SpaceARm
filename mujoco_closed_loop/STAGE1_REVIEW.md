# Stage 1: SUPER + Qingyu narrow-gap scene review

Run `./mujoco_closed_loop/run.sh stage1` from the repository root. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, press 1 for the whole course, 2 for the robot, 3 for the obstacle course, 4 for the 90-degree bent arm inside gate 2, R to reset, SPACE to toggle the arm-bending demonstration, and Q to close. `--snapshot` writes overview, robot detail, front camera alignment, course, bent gate 2, and both camera views without opening a window. Each run archives `scene.xml` under `runs/*_stage1_review/`.

The course follows the annotated experiment's task logic: start and return basket -> three solid narrow apertures with alternating x positions -> staggered cylindrical obstacles -> pickup target. A floor route and dark portal frames make that order visible. The aperture widths are 0.72, 0.68, and 0.70 m; their clear heights are 0.59, 0.56, and 0.57 m. Panels render translucent for inspection; the collision geometry remains solid. These are design dimensions, not measured dimensions from the photo. The arm demo is a fixed-airframe physics demonstration, not an autonomous traversal.

Run `PYTHONPATH=.mujoco_deps:mujoco_closed_loop python3 mujoco_closed_loop/stage1_aperture_audit.py` to check the openings with MuJoCo signed geometry distances. The fixed preview body is welded to the world, so ordinary `data.ncon` is not a valid clearance test for it. The audit samples aircraft altitude and lateral position with a straight arm, then sweeps a 90-degree bent arm 0.7 m through each gate. Straight-arm best signed margins are -22.7, -27.0, and -22.7 mm, meaning penetration at every sampled altitude. Bent-arm sweep margins after the final camera relocation are +31.9, +16.9, and +21.9 mm. The audit covers geometry at the gate, not a full flight trajectory or controller.

The aircraft visual comes from the complete archived SUPER simulator assembly `vendor/SUPER/mars_uav_sim/perfect_drone_sim/meshes/yunque-M.dae`, converted with its assembly transform and original material groups by `convert_super_assembly.py`. Conversion hashes and mesh sizes are in `vendor/super_converted/full_assembly/provenance.json`. The separate official SUPER-Hardware STEP carbon boards remain archived and are used for provisional mass placement; they are hidden visually to avoid doubling the full assembly. Flight dynamics and rotor actuator positions have **not** yet been realigned to the displayed motors, so this model is not yet an authorized flying robot. The aircraft graphic includes its original top LiDAR. A compact forward D435 is mounted on the center front board below the propeller plane, aimed along the narrow-gap corridor.

The archived Qingyu Xacro provides the `A01Base.STL` base and `gripper.STEP.stl` three-finger CAD. The SDK YAML gives a 0.28 m single-section arc, 0.0704 m visual base offset, and an RViz backbone of 15 black spheres with 0.05 m diameter. I opened the installed ROS launch with the slider, API bridge and MCU sender disabled and inspected the actual `/continuum_arm` marker messages; this stage-1 model now derives the sphere count and size directly from that YAML and attaches the markers to the 8-unit physical bending model. The physical dynamics remain an approximation because the SDK Xacro has only a fixed base-to-gripper link and no soft-body dynamics. The gripper shell remains the original Qingyu CAD, with visual cyan/red pad covers and unchanged contact pads.

Both visible D435 bodies use the 37-part native CAD D435 assembly from the user-supplied `mujoco素材/22_全驱无人机柔性臂MuJoCo.zip`. Extracted meshes, per-part colors, and archive SHA-256 are under `vendor/reference_install/`. The supplied end-effector uses a compact three-piece saddle and two bolts; those dimensions are reproduced here. The wrist D435 remains at its original height with a downward optical axis. Its housing length runs along the aircraft x axis, parallel to the body D435. To shorten the visible support, the wrist mount moved 25 mm toward the arm; its plate length is now 60 mm rather than 85 mm, parallel to the body camera support plate. The airframe D435 remains forward-looking. The supplied `venv.zip` contains a Windows Python environment, not mounting geometry, and was not needed for the model. Camera poses still require physical calibration before sensor-driven flight.

The provisional mass model totals 2.21 kg. Component masses and mounting offsets need actual assembly measurement. No fiducial boards are placed in this scene. SLAM and autonomous traversal remain later stages.
